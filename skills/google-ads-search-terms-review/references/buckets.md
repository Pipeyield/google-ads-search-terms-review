# Search buckets

The assistant reads each search in the context of what the person says they sell and where they sell it. Cost and clicks tell you what happened in the report. They do not determine intent.

| Bucket | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer | The words suggest someone looking to buy an offering the business sells. |
| Off-target | Use off-target when the search and the supplied business context clearly indicate an intent the offering does not serve. A word such as "free" can also describe something the business offers, such as a free estimate. |
| Unsure | The search has more than one plausible meaning or the person's business details leave the intent unclear. Leave its spend out of the exclusion estimate. |

For a plumbing company, “emergency plumber near me” may be a buyer search and “plumber apprentice jobs” may be off-target. “Free quote” could make sense for a company that gives free estimates. “Plumbing advice” may be unclear without more context.

A bucket describes apparent intent. A conversion, literal bid keyword, brand name or obvious product term can protect a row even if its bucket is off-target. Write each exclusion reason as a sentence the person can check using the search and their knowledge of the business.
